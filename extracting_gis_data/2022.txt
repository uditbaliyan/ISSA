August 2022
Landslides in Gudalur Taluk, Nilgiri District, Tamil Nadu on 18th August 2022
Two land subsidences were reported from Gudalur taluk in Nilgiri district of Tamil Nadu (11.497633 N, 76.497433 E and 11.496999 N, 76.514739 E) on 18th August 2022. Around 15 houses got damaged as a result of the subsidence and creep in Nadu Gudalur. NH-181 road near Gudalur was partially damaged and subsided during this event. The first incidence is a complex type of slide, which initiated as cut slope failure, slope modification and anthropogenic activities led to the subsidence and creep while the second subsidence on NH-181 was caused due to Rainwater ingress over a prolonged period and blockage of drainage channel leading to ground cracks and subsidence.

Assessment of Landslide Prone Areas under Sohbar Sirdarship, East Khasi Hills District, Meghalaya August 2022
A preliminary assessment of landslide prone areas (25°12’47.6” N; 91°45’2.4” E) under Sohbar Sirdarship, East Khasi Hills District, Meghalaya was carried out on 30th August 2022. During this study, the vulnerability of the slope areas and the risk associated with it has been evaluated and suitable stability measures as per the site conditions were suggested.

Landslides/ Slope Failures along Major Road Corridors and Settlement Areas of Darjeeling Himalaya, West Bengal
A service item was taken up for FS 2022-2023 to collect information on new/emergent or reactivated landslides and related damages along critical communication routes, settlements, gap areas, and newly constructed road corridors in the Darjeeling Himalayas. An inventory of 364 landslides has been prepared along the major road corridors of the Darjeeling Himalayas (period of occurrence 2019 – 2022). Majority of these slides have either taken place afresh or got reactivated. Nature and occurrence of slope forming material, degree of slope and unscientific slope modification are the preparatory factors whereas excess pore pressure built up due to incessant rainfall is assessed to be the triggering factor for the debris slides. The adverse orientation of the discontinuities with respect to the slope led to planar/wedge failure which resulted in rock fall/slide. Most of the landslides that occurred in the study area are triggered by incessant heavy rainfall.

June 2022
Landslide at Jibalgre, West Garo Hills District, Meghalaya on 9th June 2022
A debris slide occurred at Jibalgre village (25°22’53.88” N; 90°03’37.22” E), West Garo Hills District, Meghalaya on 9th June 2022. The slide destroyed one house and killed three persons while injuring two people. Incessant rainfall from 5th to 10th June 2022 triggered the slide. Unplanned hill slope cutting coupled with oversaturation of the highly weathered slope forming materials is the major causative factor for this slide.

Landslide at Laitlarem, East Khasi Hills District, Meghalaya on 15th June 2022
Three landslides (Debris slides) occurred at Laitlarem village under Mawphlang Community and Rural Development Block, East Khasi Hills District, Meghalaya on 15th June, 2022 (25°26’54.06” N and 91°35’29.41” E; 25° 26' 50.77'' N and 91° 35' 27.74'' E) and (25° 26' 49.78'' N and 91° 35' 26.44'' E). The area experienced heavy and incessant rainfall in the preceding days which is attributed as the triggering factor for these debris slides. Four lives were lost and five persons injured during this event. Total destruction of the house was also noticed. Unplanned hill slope cutting for road construction coupled with oversaturation of highly weathered slope forming materials are the major causative factors.

Landslide at Lumshnong, East Jaintia Hills District, Meghalaya on 15th June 2022
A debris slide occurred at Lumshnong area (25°09’50.42 N & 92°23’13.3 E) located on the NH-6 road, East Jaintia Hills district of Meghalaya on 15th June 2022. A part of road has slid down which has resulted into blockage of communication along National Highway-6, triggered by heavy rainfall in the preceding days. An artificial depression had formed due to the unsystematic dumping of quarry material in the upslope portion where rain water accumulated. The sudden release of this water led to the oversaturation of the filled material and caused a portion of the road below to wash away.

Landslide at Siju, South Garo Hills District, Meghalaya on 17th June 2022
A Debris Slide triggered by incessant rainfall has occurred at Siju (25°21’39.72” N; 90°38’49.23” E), South Garo Hills, Meghalaya on 17th June 2022. The occurrence of this landslide was attributed to the factors viz. i) Unscientific slope cutting without adequate toe support and ii) Oversaturation of highly weathered slope forming material during the heavy downpour. It was reported that one person was killed and two kids were injured in this landslide event.

Landslide at Baghmara, South Garo Hills District, Meghalaya on 17th June 2022
Two debris slides occurred at Baghmara (25°12’45.86” N; 90°37’55.78” E and 25°12’36.80” N; 90°37’55.93” E), South Garo Hills, Meghalaya on 17th June 2022 triggered by incessant rainfall from 13th to 17th June 2022. The casual factors includes slope cutting without providing adequate toe support and oversaturation of slope forming material during rainfall. Three casualties along with complete destruction of a house was reported due to this landslide.

Landslide at Nongal Bibra, South Garo Hills District, Meghalaya on 17th June 2022
Two landslide events occurred at Nongal Bibra, South Garo Hills, Meghalaya at i) 25° 27' 33.83'' N latitude, 90° 42' 30.97'' E longitude and ii) 25° 27' 32.51'' N latitude, 90° 42' 29.28'' E longitude on 17th June 2022. Heavy and incessant rainfall during the period from 4th to 17th June was the triggering factor. Steeply sloping topography, toe cutting coupled with weak slope forming materials and the oversaturation of slope forming material during the heavy rainfall leads to this slope stability issue at Nongal Bibra. Four houses were reported damaged during this event.

The disastrous Tupul Railway Station landslide of 30th June, 2022 in Noney district, Manipur
The Tupul railway station was damaged by a disastrous landslide during the early hours (00:30 hrs) of 30th June, 2022 that killed 42 people including army personals, railway staffs, labours and local civilians (as on 10:00hrs, 4th July, 2022). As many as 80 people are reported to affected in the incident of which 42 are recovered dead, 18 rescued with severe fatalities and 20 missing. The landslide occurred at the construction site of the Tupul Railway station which is 50 km away from Imphal. The massive landslide extends from latitudes 24˚48ʹ 23.93ʺ N to 24˚48ʹ 08.34ʺ N and longitudes 93˚40ʹ 02.57ʺ E to 93˚ 51ʹ 9.04ʺ E.
